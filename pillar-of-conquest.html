Official Rules & Gameplay Manual

Every unit matters. Every deployment is a decision. Every turn can change the battle. Every casualty is permanent.

1. Introduction

Conquest is a two-player tactical battlefield strategy game combining military formations, strategic positioning, battlefield control and tactical decision-making. Victory depends on the commander's ability to anticipate the opponent, establish formations, exploit weaknesses and make effective decisions.

2. The Battlefield

  • The battlefield is a 10 × 10 grid — 100 squares.
  • Each square can contain a maximum of one unit.
  • Units cannot move outside the battlefield.
  • Both players may deploy units anywhere on the battlefield, provided the selected squares are unoccupied.

3. Armies — Living vs Undead

Two mirrored armies meet on the board. The Living fight in gold and crimson; the Undead rise in bone and grave-teal, with glowing eyes. Each side commands exactly 12 units: 6 infantry, 2 cavalry, 2 riflemen, 1 disruptor, 1 spy — the same unit for unit, so the battle is a fair mirror match. Each unit has its own movement, attack and tactical abilities — see sections 8–12.

The Living

Warrior ×6

A warrior with a large crimson-and-gold shield and a raised sword.

Role: Formation, defence and battlefield control.

Move: 1 square, any direction.

Attack: 1 adjacent square, any direction.

After a kill: Remains in its original square. It does not move into the defeated unit's square.

Shield Formation: 3+ in a straight line — see §8.

Knight ×2

A plumed knight with lance and shield, mounted on a living horse.

Role: Mobility, penetration and battlefield disruption.

Move: 1–2 squares, any direction. May jump over an occupied square when moving two.

Attack: 1 adjacent square only.

After a kill: Advances into the eliminated enemy's square and occupies it.

Rifleman ×2

A rifleman in a crimson coat and campaign hat, ammo belt across his chest, rifle levelled.

Role: Ranged support and battlefield control.

Move: 1 square, any direction.

Attack: 1–2 squares in any direction, including diagonally, with clear line of sight. Shield Formation blocks all shots.

After a kill: Remains in its original square. It does not move into the defeated unit's square.

Disruptor ×1

A hooded gold-and-crimson figure with an open spellbook in hand and a glowing orb of energy raised above the other.

Role: Anti-attack specialist — prevents nearby enemy units from attacking.

Move: Exactly 1 square, any direction including diagonally. Cannot finish on an occupied square.

Disruption: Every enemy unit in its eight adjacent squares cannot attack while adjacent — but can still move normally. Suppression ends the moment a unit is no longer adjacent.

Combat: Cannot attack or eliminate any unit. Cannot be eliminated by Infantry, Cavalry or Riflemen — only a Spy can eliminate it. See §11.

Spy ×1

A hooded scout in a crimson cloak with gold trim, a knife held ready in his hand.

Role: Disruptor hunter — infiltrates enemy positions to eliminate Disruptors.

Move: 1 or 2 squares, any direction including diagonally. Cannot pass through or jump over occupied squares.

Attack: Only an adjacent enemy Disruptor. A successful attack eliminates it and the Spy occupies its square. Cannot attack Infantry, Cavalry, Riflemen or other Spies.

Weakness: Can be eliminated by Infantry and Cavalry in melee. Riflemen cannot eliminate a Spy through ranged attacks. See §12.

The Undead

The Undead field the same five units with the same movement, attacks and abilities as their Living counterparts above — only their appearance differs.

Skeleton Warrior ×6

A grinning skull in a spiked gold crown, ribcage bared, carrying a tattered teal shield and a rusted sword.

Skeleton Knight ×2

A crowned skeleton knight with a lance, riding a skeleton horse — visible ribs, bone legs and a skull head with glowing eyes.

Skeleton Rifleman ×2

A hooded skeleton in a tattered grave-coat, bone hands gripping an old, rust-flecked rifle.

Skeleton Disruptor ×1

A crowned skeleton in a grave-teal robe, an open spellbook in its bone hands and a glowing teal orb raised above the other, eyes burning teal.

Skeleton Spy ×1

A hooded skeleton scout in a tattered teal cloak, a knife gripped in one bone hand, eyes glowing teal in the dark.

4. Coin Toss & Opening Choice

Before deployment, both players call heads or tails. The winner of the coin toss chooses whether to deploy first or deploy second. The winner must declare the choice before deployment begins, and it cannot be changed once declared.

Option A — Deploy First

  • The coin toss winner deploys first.
  • The winner completes their deployment before the opponent.
  • Once deployment is complete, the winner takes the first battle turn.

Advantage: First deployment and first battle action.

Option B — Deploy Second

  • The opponent deploys first; the coin toss winner deploys second.
  • The winner completes their deployment after the opponent.
  • Once deployment is complete, the opponent takes the first battle turn.

Advantage: Final deployment — you can respond to the opponent's completed formation.

Opening rules: the first deployer always takes the first battle turn, and the second deployer always takes the final deployment turn. Either way, players alternate placing exactly two units per deployment turn, and both players must deploy all 12 units before the battle begins. The coin toss winner chooses which advantage they prefer.

5. Tactical Open Deployment

Deployment is an essential part of the game — the first test of a commander.

  • Players alternate deployment turns.
  • Each deployment turn is placing exactly two units.
  • You may deploy any combination of your remaining unit types.
  • Units may be deployed anywhere on the battlefield, provided the squares are unoccupied.
  • You may observe your opponent's deployment and adapt your own positioning.
  • Once deployed, units cannot be repositioned during the deployment phase.
  • Each player completes six deployment turns, placing all 12 units.
  • The battle begins once all 24 units have been deployed.

6. Turn Structure & the 100-Turn Limit

During the battle, each player receives exactly one action per turn:

  1. Move one unit, or
  2. Attack with one unit.
  • You cannot move and attack in the same turn, even when using different units.
  • Special abilities triggered by an attack are part of that same action — they do not grant extra turns.
  • Every action passes the turn to the opponent.
  • An illegal or blocked attack consumes your turn.

The 100-turn limit — the whole game cannot stall forever.

  • The battle lasts a maximum of 100 battle turns in total. The game counts the turns for you — you will see “Battle turn X of 100” throughout the battle, with a warning as the limit approaches.
  • A battle turn is one full round: the first deployer acts, then the opponent acts. Turn 100 is the last turn — when both players have taken their action on Turn 100, the game ends immediately. There is no Turn 101.
  • If a player eliminates all opposing units before the end of Turn 100, they win at once as normal — the limit never overrides an elimination win.
  • If Turn 100 ends with units still on both sides, the player with more units remaining wins. Every unit counts the same — each is worth one, and unit type does not break the tie. If both players have the same number of units remaining, the game is a draw — there is no further tiebreak.

Plan for the deadline: a lead on units is worth protecting in the late game, and a player who is behind must force eliminations before Turn 100 runs out.

7. General Movement Rules

  • Units move horizontally, vertically or diagonally according to their individual movement rules.
  • Normal movement cannot pass through occupied squares.
  • Units cannot finish normal movement on an occupied square.
  • Units cannot leave the battlefield.
  • You cannot voluntarily move enemy units.
  • Cavalry exception: cavalry has a special jumping ability that lets it cross an occupied intervening square when moving two squares.
  • Spy exception — the reverse: a Spy also moves up to two squares, but can never jump or pass through an occupied square. If the intervening square is occupied, that route is closed.

8. Infantry (Warriors & Skeleton Warriors) — Formation, Defence & Control

Movement: 1 square in any direction, including diagonally. The destination must be unoccupied.

Attack: An enemy unit in any adjacent square, including diagonally. A successful attack eliminates the target. Infantry remains in its original square after a successful attack — it does not take the defeated unit's square.

Shield Formation: Three or more friendly infantry must form one continuous straight horizontal or vertical line:

  • At least three connected infantry units.
  • They must form a straight horizontal or vertical line. A line of more than three also qualifies.
  • L-shaped and irregular formations do not qualify.

Protection:

  • Formation members are immune to all rifleman shots — Shield Formation blocks every shot, from every direction, with no bypass. Diagonal shots do not get around it.
  • Enemy infantry and cavalry can attack formation members normally.
  • If fewer than three connected infantry remain, the formation loses its protection.

9. Cavalry (Knights & Skeleton Knights) — Mobility, Penetration & Disruption

Movement: 1 or 2 squares in any direction, including diagonally.

  • When moving two squares, cavalry may jump over an occupied intervening square.
  • The intervening unit may belong to either player.
  • The landing square must be unoccupied.
  • Jumping does not affect or displace the intervening unit.

Attack: Any enemy unit in an adjacent square, including diagonally. Cavalry cannot attack enemies two squares away. A successful attack eliminates the target, and cavalry advances into the eliminated enemy's former square.

Cavalry's jump lets it penetrate crowded formations, bypass obstacles and reposition quickly — but it never displaces a unit it passes over.

10. Riflemen — Ranged Support & Control

Movement: 1 square in any direction, including diagonally. The destination must be unoccupied.

Attack: Enemies one or two squares away in any direction — horizontally, vertically or diagonally.

  • A clear line of sight is required.
  • Any intervening unit, friendly or enemy, blocks the attack.
  • A successful attack eliminates the target.
  • Riflemen remain in their original square after a successful attack — they do not take the defeated unit's square.

Shield Formation interaction: Shield Formation blocks all rifleman shots — no shot can penetrate or bypass a valid infantry Shield Formation. A blocked attack fails and consumes your turn.

11. Disruptor — Anti-Attack Specialist

The Disruptor's role is to prevent nearby enemy units from attacking. It suppresses every adjacent enemy unit — not just Riflemen — which makes it a general anti-attack specialist rather than a dedicated rifleman counter.

Movement:

  • Moves exactly 1 square in any direction, including diagonally.
  • Cannot move through occupied squares.
  • Cannot finish on an occupied square.

Special ability — Disruption:

  • Disrupts all enemy units in its eight adjacent squares.
  • Affected units cannot attack while adjacent to the Disruptor.
  • Affected units can still move normally.
  • Suppression ends immediately when a unit is no longer adjacent.
  • Works in every direction, including diagonally.
  • Does not affect friendly units.
  • One exception: an enemy Spy is never disrupted. A Spy is trained to operate inside a Disruptor's shadow — it has to be, since standing adjacent is the only way it can strike. Every other unit type is suppressed as normal.

Combat:

  • Cannot attack or eliminate other units.
  • Cannot be eliminated by Infantry, Cavalry or Riflemen.
  • Can only be eliminated by a Spy.

On the board, a disrupted unit carries a violet burst badge: it cannot attack until it moves out of the Disruptor's shadow — or until a Spy removes the Disruptor.

12. Spy — Disruptor Hunter

The Spy's role is to infiltrate enemy positions to eliminate Disruptors — the one job no other unit can do.

Movement:

  • Moves 1 or 2 squares in any direction, including diagonally.
  • Cannot pass through occupied intervening squares.
  • Cannot jump over friendly or enemy units.
  • Cannot finish on an occupied square, except when eliminating a Disruptor.
  • Cannot leave the battlefield.

Combat:

  • Can only attack an adjacent enemy Disruptor.
  • A successful attack eliminates the Disruptor.
  • The Spy occupies the Disruptor's former square.
  • Cannot attack Infantry, Cavalry, Riflemen or other Spies.
  • Can be eliminated by all melee units, including Infantry and Cavalry.
  • Riflemen cannot eliminate Spies through ranged attacks.

The tactical loop:

  • Rifleman — attacks enemies from a distance.
  • Disruptor — prevents adjacent enemies from attacking.
  • Spy — infiltrates and eliminates Disruptors.
  • Infantry and Cavalry — can eliminate enemy Spies.

Protect your Spy until it can reach the enemy Disruptor — and hunt the enemy Spy before it reaches yours.

13. Combat & Elimination

Combat is deterministic — there are no hit points, dice rolls or random combat outcomes.

  • Every successful legal attack eliminates its target.
  • Eliminated units are permanently removed from the battlefield.
  • Every casualty permanently changes the battlefield.

After a kill — who takes the square?

  • Infantry — stays put. It remains in the square it attacked from; the defeated unit's square becomes empty.
  • Rifleman — stays put. It remains in the square it shot from, even if the target was only one square away.
  • Cavalry — advances and occupies. It leaves its own square and moves into the defeated unit's former square.
  • Spy — advances and occupies, against a Disruptor only. When a Spy eliminates a Disruptor, it moves into the Disruptor's former square — the only kill a Spy can ever make.
  • Disruptor — never attacks. It eliminates nothing; its Disruption ability is passive and costs no action.

Infantry holds. Riflemen hold. Cavalry takes the square — and so does a Spy, when it kills a Disruptor.

14. Victory Conditions & the 100-Turn Limit

A player wins by:

  • Eliminating all opposing units; or
  • Forcing the opponent to surrender; or
  • Having more units remaining when the 100-turn limit ends the game.

At the end of Turn 100, if neither player has been wiped out, the units left on the board are counted: more units remaining wins, and equal numbers is a draw. It is a straight count — every surviving unit counts as one, whatever its type, and there is no value tiebreak: a surviving Cavalry does not outrank a surviving Rifleman or Infantry at the limit (see §6).

Losing all your cavalry or riflemen does not automatically mean defeat — and losing your Disruptor or Spy does not either. A player can still win with infantry alone. No individual unit type is essential to winning.

16. Player Reminders

  • Deployment is unrestricted, provided squares are unoccupied — exactly two units per deployment turn, six turns each.
  • Each battle turn allows exactly one action — move or attack. Casualties are permanent.
  • Normal movement is blocked by occupied squares. Cavalry can jump over one when moving two squares, and never displaces the unit it passes over; a Spy can never jump.
  • After a kill, infantry and riflemen stay in their square — cavalry occupies the defeated unit's square, and so does a Spy when it eliminates a Disruptor.
  • A Disruptor cannot attack, and can only be eliminated by a Spy. Units adjacent to an enemy Disruptor cannot attack but can still move, and the suppression ends as soon as they are no longer adjacent — a Spy is never disrupted.
  • A Spy can only attack a Disruptor, and cannot be shot by Riflemen — but Infantry and Cavalry can eliminate it in melee.
  • The whole game ends after 100 battle turns. If no one has been wiped out by then, most units remaining wins — and if units are level, it is a draw.

Playstyles

Different ways to approach the battle. Pick one, combine them, or invent your own.

  • Aggressive — Push forward and attack early. Take units first and keep the pressure on.
  • Balanced — Mix attack and defence. Advance steadily while keeping your formation solid.
  • Defensive — Hold a strong position and make the enemy come to you. Let them take the risks.
  • Reactive — Wait for the enemy to commit first, then counter their plan.
  • Bait Trapping — Offer a unit that looks exposed to draw the enemy in, then punish the attack.
  • Mirror — Copy the enemy's setup and shape, then try to outplay them with it.

Make it yours: Combine styles — start Defensive and turn Aggressive, or Mirror an opening then set a Bait Trap to finish.

17. The Spirit of Conquest

Conquest is a game of tactical command and strategic planning. The deployment phase is the first test of a commander's ability. Every placement reveals an intention. Every formation creates opportunities. Every opening can become a trap.

Infantry holds the line. Cavalry breaks through. Riflemen control distance. The Disruptor silences the enemy's weapons. The Spy hunts in the shadows. Victory belongs to the commander who understands the battlefield, anticipates the opponent and makes the right decisions at the right time.
